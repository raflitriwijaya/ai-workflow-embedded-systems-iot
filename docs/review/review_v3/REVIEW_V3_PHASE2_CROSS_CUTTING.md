## Executive Summary

Phase 1 established that the lifecycle is end-to-end traversable and that the organization has converted an *unknowable* robustness void into a *bounded, owned* backlog: 36 cross-layer failure chains in [[docs/fmea/SYSTEM_FMEA_V1|SYSTEM_FMEA_V1]] (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis), 17 of them Critical (Risk Priority Number — RPN — ≥ 200) and Open, with end-to-end detection coverage honestly self-reported at ≈ 53% against a ≥ 95% target. **Phase 2 traces the concerns that cut across stages, and the headline is that the cross-cutting view does not soften the Phase 1 picture — it sharpens it into three structural deficits that no single stage owns and that therefore no single stage verdict captures.** The strongest cross-cutting concern is **security's structural embedding**: there is a named security activity, owner, and artifact at every one of the six stages, wired from the Researcher's Pre-Transfer Security Review through to the post-launch patch SLA (Service-Level Agreement). The weakest is **detection/observability**: the 15 chains scored Detectability (D) ≥ 8 are not distributed evenly — they cluster precisely on the silent, in-range, closed-loop failures that the product's core value depends on, and the nine controls that would see them "do not exist today."

**The most dangerous residual gaps are not the ones the FMEA lists — they are the ones the cross-cutting trace reveals that the FMEA's RPN frame cannot price.** Three surface in Phase 2 that survived both Part 1 (static analysis) and Part 2 (the prior simulation). First, **the Security Engineer is a genuine single point of failure**: every Security-Relevant release sign-off (and AgriSpectra's OTA — Over-the-Air — , cryptographic, and new-product releases are *all* Security-Relevant by the §7.1 definition) is **non-delegable** to the Deputy, so the entire blocking authority for the most consequential releases rests on one human who also owns post-launch vulnerability watch, threat modeling across seven implementing roles, and incident response. Second, **the OTA incident machinery is crash-shaped, not erosion-shaped**: the [[docs/operations/INCIDENT_COMMANDER|Incident Commander]] (IC) declaration triggers fire on FAILED-state rates and Service-Level-Objective (SLO) breaches — loud failures — while the keystone FC-022 closed-loop corruption produces *no crash, no SLO breach, no FAILED report*, so the best-resourced response function in the ecosystem is structurally blind to the worst failure in the product. Third, **physics caps the incident response**: a Critical firmware patch to a 50,000-device LoRaWAN (Long Range Wide Area Network) fleet cannot physically traverse the downlink duty cycle inside the 7-day Critical remediation SLA, and a physical vulnerability (FC-028, unlocked debug port on a production sub-batch) cannot be patched OTA *at all*.

**AI agent readiness is real but uneven, and its single enabler is unbuilt.** Roles whose deliverables are backed by the eight machine-parseable schemas (Architecture Decision Record — ADR, Contract Clarification Record — CCR, Data Quality Issue Report — DQIR, Integration Readiness Declaration — IRD, OTA Compatibility Manifest — OCM, Security Implementation Readiness Checklist — SIRC, Technology Transfer Pack — TTP, Business Impact Assessment — BIA) are genuinely agent-executable at Tiers 3–4 today; roles gated by physical validation (board bring-up, Hardware-in-the-Loop — HIL, Electromagnetic Compatibility) and by Tier-1 human judgment (the Security veto, the Architect production gate, the adversarial FMEA itself) are not, and should not be. But the coordination layer that would let agents exchange those schemas at machine speed — the [[docs/agent-protocol/MULTI_AGENT_COORDINATION_PROTOCOL|Multi-Agent Coordination Protocol]] (MACP) — is a draft specification with a four-wave rollout that has not started; its registries are not stood up. **The pending prompts are sufficient *as designs* to close the known gaps, but "closed by specification" is doing heavy lifting**: of the 14, the ones that close Critical chains (the nine FMEA §7.1 controls, the F1 OTA watchdog, the FC-022 ground-truth anchor) are named-and-owned but *unbuilt*, and a design that describes a control is not a control. The cross-cutting interim verdict is therefore **CONDITIONAL — the same shape as every Phase 1 stage verdict from S3 onward**: structurally sound, honestly mapped, finite path to GO, not GO today.

## 1. Security Across the Lifecycle

Security is the cross-cutting concern the ecosystem does **best**, structurally — and the one whose central mechanism hides the ecosystem's most under-acknowledged single point of failure. This section traces it stage by stage, naming the owner, the produced artifact, and the artifact the next stage consumes, then confronts the capacity question directly.

This is a **complete chain with no orphaned hand-off**: every stage's security artifact is consumed by the next, and the veto is retained through S6 ("Retain release veto authority until the patch is verified deployed," §3.6). The Incident Commander charter explicitly preserves the veto's primacy — "the Security Engineer's release veto is never overridable by the Incident Commander" ([[docs/operations/INCIDENT_COMMANDER|IC §5.2]]). That is a genuine structural strength: the one authority that can stop a bad release survives even emergency tempo. #strength

### 1.2 The Gaps Between Stages

- **S1→S2 disclosure-dependence (HA-H1).** The Pre-Transfer Security Review fires only because the *Researcher tags* the finding `#sensor-physics`/`#connectivity` (§6.10). A genuinely novel surface the Researcher does not recognize as security-relevant is never briefed. FC-029 (a "lying" tampered device presenting valid mTLS identity, D = 9) is the realized descendant of exactly this surface — and it is detectable only by attestation, which does not yet exist (§1.4). #gap #risk
- **S3 self-attestation gap.** Security Implementation Readiness is completed by each team's *own* Security Champion (a first-line reviewer, explicitly "NOT a deputy … cannot sign off"). The SIRC schema validates evidence existence but cannot validate that a *per-unit production* control was applied. **FC-028** (unlocked debug port on a manufacturing sub-batch, RPN 243, D = 9, Open—MANDATORY) is "per-design, not per-batch": a self-attested design-time checklist structurally cannot catch a per-batch production miss, and there is **no contracted audit-sampling** of the self-attested gates. The Phase 1 S3 finding stands. #gap #risk
- **S5→S6 re-entry latency.** A Security veto on the OTA *model* path (FC-026, the Phase 1 S5 veto) is clean, but no card specifies how fast a re-signed, re-validated model release re-enters the gate. For a disease-pattern update with a *seasonal* cost, the absence of a re-entry SLA converts a correct veto into an open-ended hold. #gap

### 1.3 Capacity: Can One Security Engineer Review All Seven Implementing Roles? — **No, Not For Security-Relevant Releases**

The role card asks the Security Engineer to *define and verify* the baseline across **seven implementing surfaces**: [[FIRMWARE_ENGINEER_SKILL|Firmware]], [[HARDWARE_ENGINEER_SKILL|Hardware]], [[BACKEND_CLOUD_ENGINEER_SKILL|Backend]], [[DEVOPS_PLATFORM_ENGINEER_SKILL|DevOps]], [[MLOPS_ENGINEER_SKILL|MLOps]], [[DATA_ENGINEER_SKILL|Data]], and [[EDGE_AI_ML_ENGINEER_SKILL|Edge ML]] (model integrity). The card *knows* this is a bottleneck — it tags §7.1 `#bottleneck` — and offers two relief valves:

1. **Security Champions** (one per implementing role) as first-line reviewers and "early-warning sensors." But the card is explicit: a Champion "cannot sign off on releases or modify the security baseline." They reduce review *volume*; they do not carry review *authority*.
2. **The Deputy Security Engineer**, who can sign off **Standard-tier releases only**.

Here is the brutal arithmetic the design does not state plainly. For a **Security-Relevant** release, §7.1 requires that the Security Engineer (a) conduct a threat-model review of the changed surface, (b) commission penetration testing if network-facing, and (c) perform a **Security Engineer-conducted review (explicitly not self-assessment)** of the relevant roles' conformance — **and this is "not delegable to Deputy."** A release is **Security-Relevant** if it touches OTA, a new protocol, a new data flow across a trust boundary, cryptography, authentication, new PII (Personally Identifiable Information) handling, **or is the first production release of a new product or major version.**

**Every release AgriSpectra will make for its entire first year is Security-Relevant by that definition.** The first launch is a new product. The disease-pattern model updates traverse the OTA path. Any LoRaWAN session-security change is cryptographic. Therefore the Deputy and the Champions — the two designed relief valves — **cannot absorb any of the load-bearing work**: the Deputy is locked out of Security-Relevant sign-off, and the Champions cannot sign at all. The entire weight of every consequential release review falls on one human, who is *simultaneously* contracted to run continuous post-launch vulnerability monitoring (1-business-hour Critical SLA), the quarterly Joint Data Security & Governance Review, threat-derived test-case authoring on a 10-business-day SLA, and incident-response participation. **This is a single point of failure, and it is structural, not a staffing accident.** It is the same person, the same week, for the release gate, the incident, and the post-launch watch. The honest mitigation is not "hire a Champion" (already done) — it is either (a) expand the Deputy's authority to cover a defined subset of Security-Relevant reviews under audit, or (b) accept a hard throughput ceiling on Security-Relevant releases and schedule against it. Neither is in the corpus. #gap #critical

### 1.4 The Attestation Dependency

Two of the most dangerous security-adjacent chains — FC-029 (lying device) and the trust-the-device-reports assumption HA-A3 — are addressed *only* by the [[docs/security/DEVICE_ATTESTATION_SPEC|Device Attestation Specification]], which is **draft, status "Long-Term Bet."** Its Phase 1 (firmware + boot-state attestation via RATS/EAT/DICE — Remote ATtestation procedureS / Entity Attestation Token / Device Identifier Composition Engine) **requires no hardware change** and is buildable on the existing secure element, MCUboot, and PKI. That it is buildable today and still unbuilt is the crux: the design has correctly identified that mTLS authenticates *identity*, not *truth*, and has specified the fix — but until it ships, the fleet plane still reconciles against what devices *say*, and FC-029 remains D = 9. #gap

## 2. OTA Governance End-to-End

### 2.1 Normal OTA Flow

A model OTA traverses a closed loop **MLO → DEV → FW → BACK → MLO** ([[MLOPS_ENGINEER_SKILL|MLOps]] → [[DEVOPS_PLATFORM_ENGINEER_SKILL|DevOps]] → [[FIRMWARE_ENGINEER_SKILL|Firmware]] → [[BACKEND_CLOUD_ENGINEER_SKILL|Backend]] → MLOps), governed by the **OTA Model Artifact Contract** and the per-model [[docs/schemas/OTA_COMPATIBILITY_MANIFEST_SCHEMA|OTA Compatibility Manifest]] (OCM). Hop by hop:

| Hop | Actor | Action | Gate / Artifact | SLA / Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [[MLOPS_ENGINEER_SKILL\|MLOps]] | Train → validate → quantize → package → **register**; emit OCM (model SHA-256, framework, quantization, firmware-compat range, flash/RAM budget check, signing key + signature, `dqir_clearance`) | OCM validates (16 rules); `flash_budget_check.result` must not be FAIL; DQIRs resolved | Registered |
| 2 | [[DEVOPS_PLATFORM_ENGINEER_SKILL\|DevOps]] | Co-sign, package for transport, **distribute** to edge endpoint; staged/cohort rollout | Status → `DISTRIBUTING` → `DISTRIBUTED` | Per-cohort |
| 3 | [[BACKEND_CLOUD_ENGINEER_SKILL\|Backend]] | Set device-twin `desired` **only after `DISTRIBUTED`** confirmed (FC-020 barrier) | Twin `desired` set | Twin updated |
| 4 | [[FIRMWARE_ENGINEER_SKILL\|Firmware]] | Receive `DESIRED_SET` via LoRaWAN downlink; download; **verify signature chain**; on-device flash-budget + compatibility check; sanity-check inference; promote to **ACTIVE** (MCUboot A/B) | Signature verify (FC-026), compat manifest (FC-033/FC-036), sanity check (FW §6.4) | `DOWNLOADING` → `ACTIVE` |
| 5 | [[BACKEND_CLOUD_ENGINEER_SKILL\|Backend]] | Reconcile `reported == ACTIVE(new)`; twin desired-vs-reported drift monitor (>1%/1h) | Twin reconciliation | Reported ACTIVE |
| 6 | [[MLOPS_ENGINEER_SKILL\|MLOps]] | Registry status → live; fleet-status monitor ("stuck non-ACTIVE > 1h") | Registry update | Loop closed |

The **chain-level timeout owner is Backend**, which holds the end-to-end OTA transaction wall-clock (24 h staged / 1 h hotfix). [[QA_TEST_AUTOMATION_ENGINEER_SKILL|QA]]'s end-to-end OTA validation scenario exercises apply + rollback on real hardware (S4). **The single source of truth is real**: the OCM is the one canonical format MLOps conforms to, DevOps packages, Firmware verifies, Backend references — any change requires an ADR naming all four as consulted.

**Where the normal flow is already thin.** The chain-level timeout has **no owner on the pre-`DESIRED_SET` hops** — the Backend twin-sync and MLOps "stuck > 1h" monitors both live *downstream* of `DESIRED_SET` (Phase 1 finding F1). FC-014 (downlink duty-cycle drops the desired-version command) and FC-031 (DevOps stall in `DISTRIBUTING`) both produce a twin showing `desired == reported == old`, i.e. **no mismatch, no alert, campaign silently dead** — both Open—MANDATORY, both D = 8. The F1 chain-level watchdog that would close this is in the "does-not-exist-today" set. So the *normal* OTA flow is well-specified for hops 3–6 and unwatched for hops 1–3. #gap

### 2.2 OTA Failure Scenario — Model Passes All Checks, Canary Crashes 10% of the Fleet

This is the scenario where claimed robustness meets reality. **Setup:** MLOps registers model v4.3.0; the OCM validates (signature valid, flash budget within profile, firmware-compat range correct, DQIRs cleared); QA's pre-deployment OTA + rollback scenario passes on the representative HIL rig. DevOps distributes to a 5% canary cohort. On real field hardware across the fleet's hardware-revision and temperature diversity, the model triggers a **watchdog-reset crash loop on 10% of canary devices** — a path the representative rig did not exercise.

**Trace the response, step by step, naming owner and SLA at each hop:**

1. **Firmware detects (device-local).** MCUboot A/B + watchdog-driven revert ([[FIRMWARE_ENGINEER_SKILL|FW]] §4.6, §6.7): repeated crashes within the boot-confirmation window trigger automatic revert to slot B. Device reports `ROLLED_BACK` within 30 s. **Owner: Firmware. SLA: ≤ 30 s on-device (Architect NFR R3, Class B ≤ 120 s).** ✅ This works for a *crash*.
2. **The rollback can itself silently corrupt (FC-033).** If firmware has advanced since the slot-B model was current, the rolled-back **model + current firmware** mismatch on arena size / preprocessing → device runs but inference is silently wrong. Compatibility is checked at *apply*, not re-validated at *rollback*. R1's containment invariant says the device must fail to a **SUPPRESS** state, not an incompatible model — **but FC-033 is Open—MANDATORY, D = 9.** So the safety mechanism's own failure mode is unmitigated. **This is the first break point.** #critical
3. **Backend detects the fleet signal.** Twin reported-state flips to `ROLLED_BACK` across the canary; desired-vs-reported drift monitor fires (>1% in 1h). **Owner: Backend.** ✅
4. **Incident Commander declaration.** Campaign failure rate 10% > the 5%-FAILED threshold in [[docs/operations/INCIDENT_COMMANDER|IC §4.1]] → an incident **must** be declared. Any Senior/Staff engineer (or auto-monitoring) posts to `#incident-active`; on-duty IC acknowledges within **15 minutes**, convenes the war room within **30 minutes**. **Owner: Incident Commander.** ✅
5. **DevOps halts distribution.** IC directs DevOps to pause the staged rollout (within existing authority; no new budget needed, IC §5.1). **Owner: DevOps.** ✅ — *but* FC-032 (stuck partial rollout, no max-dwell timer) means a *paused* stage is not distinguished from *in-progress*; if the halt notification is lost the fleet stays split across versions. High, mitigate.
6. **MLOps updates the registry.** Model v4.3.0 marked bad; rollback target pinned. **Owner: MLOps.** ✅
7. **IC coordinates the cross-layer response** under emergency tempo (15-min status cadence, decision logging within 5 min). An emergency corrective OTA may be declared — **but the Security release gate remains required and non-overridable** (IC §5.2). Because a corrective release touching the OTA path is Security-Relevant, **the non-delegable Security Engineer sign-off (§1.3) is now on the critical path of the incident.** #risk
8. **Post-incident review** scheduled within 5 business days (IC §6.2); every waived ADR documented retroactively within 5 business days; QA/Process Architect files the incident report to the ARB.
9. **FMEA learning loop.** Update trigger (c): "any production incident that reveals a failure chain not in this inventory — **mandatory same-cycle addition**" ([[docs/fmea/SYSTEM_FMEA_V1|FMEA §8]]). If the crash path is a new chain, it is added. **Owner: Architect (FMEA owner), QA (validator).** ✅
10. **Research Re-Entry — only if root cause is fundamental.** If the crash traces to a model that learned a sensor-physics artifact, the fix routes to [[IOT_EMBEDDED_SYSTEMS_RESEARCHER_SKILL|Research]] via Reverse Technology Transfer (§3.7, 30-business-day initiation). **But this is Researcher-pull, quarterly-cadence, and the field-push B5 classify-and-route control does not exist.** #gap

**Verdict on the failure scenario: the loud-crash case works; the silent cases — which are the dangerous ones — break.** Every hop in steps 1, 3–9 has an owner and an SLA, and the IC machinery is genuinely well-formed. But the scenario as posed (a 10% **crash** rate) is the *easy* case precisely because it is loud: watchdog resets, FAILED reports, and a > 5% rate are all detectable, so the IC trigger fires. The failures the FMEA actually fears do **not** present this way:

- **The IC triggers are crash-shaped, not erosion-shaped.** A model that degrades accuracy 10% *without crashing* produces no watchdog reset, no FAILED state, no SLO breach. The drift monitor re-baselines against the corruption (FC-022). **No IC declaration criterion is met, so the best-resourced response function in the ecosystem never activates.** The 10%-crash scenario passes; the 10%-silent-erosion scenario sails straight through. #critical
- **FC-031 inverts the trigger logic.** If the campaign stalls *before* `DESIRED_SET`, devices never attempt the update, so they never report FAILED — the "campaign failure rate" the IC keys on never rises, because the devices are silently *absent* from the campaign, not failing it. The trigger assumes devices report; the failure mode is that they were never told.
- **FC-033 means rollback is not a safe harbor.** Step 2 above: reverting can create the very silent corruption the rollback was meant to prevent.

So the OTA loop's claimed robustness is **real for detectable failures and absent for silent ones**, and the silent ones (FC-022, FC-031, FC-033, FC-008) are exactly the Open—MANDATORY Critical chains. The IC is a correctly-designed coordinator pointed at the wrong class of failure.

## 4. AI Agent Readiness Per Lifecycle Stage

The corpus is unusually agent-ready in three respects: every role card carries a §9 AI Agent Execution Guide; eight deliverables are backed by machine-parseable schemas (the [[docs/schemas/SCHEMA_INDEX|Schema Index]]); and the [[docs/agent-protocol/MULTI_AGENT_COORDINATION_PROTOCOL|MACP]] defines a four-tier authority model that draws the human/agent boundary explicitly (Tier 1 = permanent Human-in-the-Loop — HITL; Tiers 2–4 = agent-coordinable). It is unready in one decisive respect: **MACP is a draft whose registries are not stood up (Wave 1 not started), so the machine-speed coordination that would let agents exchange these schemas does not yet operate.** Agent readiness today is therefore *per-role-in-isolation*, not *coordinated*.

The table is specific per role per stage. "Ready" = an agent within its §2 scope, §9 guide, and §6 contracts could execute the activity today, producing a schema-valid artifact, at Tier 3–4. "Requires human" = Tier 1 judgment, physical validation, or adversarial creativity.

| Stage | Roles Ready for Agent Execution (specific) | Roles Requiring Human (specific) | Key Enablers Needed |
|---|---|---|---|

## 5. Governance Under Stress

### 5.1 Scenario A — A Critical Security Vulnerability in Production

**Setup.** Eight months post-launch, a security researcher responsibly discloses two findings against the AgriSpectra fleet: (i) a remotely-triggerable memory-corruption CVE in the device-side TLS stack (mbedTLS), exploitable over the LoRaWAN session to achieve code execution; and (ii) evidence that a manufacturing sub-batch (~3,000 units) shipped with the JTAG/SWD debug port **unlocked** (the realized FC-028).

**Step-by-step response chain:**

1. **Detection.** The disclosure arrives via the responsible-disclosure channel and/or the SBOM (Software Bill of Materials) advisory feed the Security Engineer monitors post-launch ([[SECURITY_ENGINEER_SKILL|SEC]] §3.6: investigate Critical within **1 business hour**). **Owner: Security Engineer.**
2. **Classification & declaration.** Security scores the TLS CVE Critical (CVSS ≥ 9.0). A confirmed, exploitable compromise vector meets the IC declaration criterion "confirmed or high-confidence compromise of device/firmware" ([[docs/operations/INCIDENT_COMMANDER|IC §4.1]]). IC declares, acknowledges within **15 minutes**, war room within **30 minutes**. **Owner: Incident Commander.**
3. **Advisory issuance.** Security issues advisories to Firmware/Backend/DevOps with remediation SLAs: **Critical = 7 days** (§3.6). **Owner: Security Engineer.**
4. **The two findings diverge — and one cannot be patched OTA at all.** The TLS CVE is a firmware fix (FW bumps mbedTLS, DevOps re-signs the image). **The unlocked debug port (FC-028) is physical: it cannot be remediated by OTA.** The ~3,000 affected units require RMA (Return Merchandise Authorization) or a field accept-the-risk decision. **This is the first hard friction point: a Critical vulnerability with no software remedy.** A fleet-wide "patch" closes half the incident; the physical half is an RMA campaign on a season-deployed fleet, which for an agricultural product may be operationally impossible until harvest. #critical
5. **The corrective release is Security-Relevant — and routes back through the single Security Engineer.** A post-incident security fix is *automatically* Security-Relevant (§7.1) and the sign-off is **not delegable to the Deputy.** The same human now commanding/advising the incident must also conduct the threat-model review and conformance review of the patch and sign the emergency release. **Second friction point: §1.3's single point of failure becomes the incident's critical path**, and the §7.1 Security-Relevant sign-off SLA (10 business days) *exceeds* the Critical remediation SLA (7 days) — the emergency-OTA path (IC §5.1) compresses tempo but the threat-model + pentest of the changed surface still must occur. The two SLAs are in tension and the corpus does not reconcile them. #gap
6. **The emergency OTA meets LoRaWAN physics.** IC declares an emergency OTA (with Security gate intact, IC §5.2). DevOps runs a 1-hour-hotfix-timeout staged rollout. **But a 50,000-device LoRaWAN fleet cannot receive a full firmware image inside 7 days**: downlink duty-cycle limits (the root of FC-014) bound how fast firmware bytes can reach devices, and a firmware image is far larger than a desired-state command. **Third friction point: the Critical 7-day SLA is physically unachievable for a firmware patch over LoRaWAN at fleet scale**, independent of any process. Compounding: FC-035 thundering-herd if devices reconnect en masse; FC-004 anti-rollback-counter wear on aged units; FC-031 silent campaign stall pre-`DESIRED_SET`. #critical
7. **Post-incident formalization.** Retroactive ADRs within 5 business days; post-incident review scheduled; FMEA mandatory same-cycle addition if the CVE reveals a new chain (it confirms FC-028 and likely adds a "TLS-stack RCE" chain). **Owner: Architect (FMEA), QA/Process Architect (report).**

**Scenario A verdict.** The *governance* chain is correct and fast — detection SLA, IC declaration, veto integrity, retroactive formalization all fire as designed. The *physics and capacity* are where it breaks: (a) physical vulns have no OTA remedy; (b) LoRaWAN downlink cannot move firmware to 50k devices in 7 days; (c) the non-delegable Security sign-off is both the incident's critical path and slower (10 d) than the remediation SLA (7 d). None of these are process failures — they are the incident response colliding with the device class's physical reality and the one-Security-Engineer bottleneck. **The org will *coordinate* a Critical vuln flawlessly and still be unable to *patch* it inside SLA.**

### 5.2 Scenario B — The Architect Is Unavailable for 4 Weeks During Development

**Setup.** Mid-S3, the [[EMBEDDED_SYSTEMS_ARCHITECT_SKILL|Architect]] is unreachable for 4 weeks. The Deputy Architect (`ARCH-DEP`, **non-breaking ADR authority only**, [[docs/agent-protocol/MULTI_AGENT_COORDINATION_PROTOCOL|MACP §3]] / Agent Identity §6) and the Architecture Review Board (ARB) must hold the line.

**Step-by-step:**

1. **What the Deputy + ARB *can* handle.** The bi-weekly shift-left ARB reviews continue (Deputy chairs; ARB quorum present). Non-breaking ADRs, routine contract interpretations, and CCRs within scope are resolved by the Deputy at Tier 3. Integration Readiness Declarations are co-signed by the consuming role + QA (Architect is a consumer of the IRD, not the sole signatory). Weekly integration smoke tests and the ≥ 2-week-failure block continue mechanically. **Most of Development proceeds.** ✅
2. **What *stalls* — and waits 4 weeks.** Any **breaking-change ADR** (a frozen-contract modification) exceeds the Deputy's authority. Per the MACP tier model these are Tier 2 (ARB-resolvable by quorum → non-binding recommendation → human ratification). **The ARB can deliberate but the binding ratification needs a human with the authority** — and if that human is the Architect, the decision waits. The **ARB expansion ("Long-Term Bet")** that would let the ARB ratify a defined class of Tier-2 architecture decisions in the Architect's absence **is a pending, unexecuted prompt** (§6) — so today the ARB's authority is *deliberative, not ratifying* for the hardest decisions. #gap
3. **The load-bearing stall: FMEA ownership.** The FMEA names a **single accountable owner — the Architect** ([[docs/fmea/SYSTEM_FMEA_V1|FMEA §8]]: "single accountable owner of system robustness"). If S3 development surfaces a *new* cross-layer chain (update trigger b/c), there is **no one with the authority to own a robustness-contract revision** for 4 weeks. The Deputy's non-breaking authority does not extend to re-baselining the System Robustness Contract. A newly-discovered Critical chain during the Architect's absence has nowhere to land. #gap #risk
4. **The single-hub strain becomes visible.** Phase 1 flagged that Planning Integration has no defined arbitration when two plans are mutually incompatible, and that inference-output-semantics ownership is split — both implicitly route to the Architect. With the Architect out, these latent single-hub dependencies have no fallback. The Deputy can interpret; the Deputy cannot *arbitrate a structural conflict.*

**Scenario B verdict.** **4 weeks is survivable; the structure does not collapse — but it reveals that the Architect is a single hub whose absence freezes exactly the decisions that matter most** (breaking changes, FMEA ownership, structural arbitration). The Deputy + ARB cover Tier 3 and deliberate Tier 2, which keeps routine Development moving. The hard stops are STRATEGIC/breaking ADRs and system-robustness ownership, which simply *wait*. **4 weeks waits; 4 months would not survive** — the queued structural decisions would dam the pipeline. The ARB expansion prompt is the correct fix and its non-execution is the gap this scenario exposes. The verdict: **resilient to a short absence, fragile to a long one, and dependent on a pending prompt to be genuinely robust.**

### 5.3 Scenario C — A Supplier Discontinues the Spectral Sensor

**Setup.** The novel spectral sensor — the product's scientific differentiator and the origin of FC-001 — reaches end-of-life (EOL); the supplier discontinues it. A replacement exists with *different* near-infrared bands, a *different* analog front-end, and a *different* I²C/SPI register map.

**Step-by-step cascade:**

1. **Trigger & classification.** Hardware supply-chain monitoring or [[BUSINESS_CONSULTANT_SKILL|Business]] market tracking flags the EOL; it enters the [[PRODUCT_OWNER_TECHNICAL_PROJECT_MANAGER_SKILL|PO/TPM]] Sustaining Engineering backlog. Because it changes a *platform* element, it is a **STRATEGIC ADR** with a [[docs/schemas/BUSINESS_IMPACT_ASSESSMENT_SCHEMA|BIA]] attached (new-sensor cost vs the affordability ceiling — re-triggering the S1 Business CONDITIONAL on BOM cost). **Owner: Architect (ADR) + Business (BIA) + ARB.**
2. **Is it a Research Re-Entry?** The new sensor's spectral physics differ, so the Researcher's original acceptance criteria (SNR — Signal-to-Noise Ratio — ≥ 45 dB under field conditions) must be **re-validated**. This is the research-assumption-validation / Reverse-Technology-Transfer path (RES §3.7). **But that path is Researcher-pull on a quarterly cadence**, and the Research-to-Planning gate runs quarterly (first Tuesday of Feb/May/Aug/Nov). A supplier EOL is *time-urgent*; the quarterly metabolism (EN-2) can impose up to a 3-month latency, and for a seasonally-deployed product that can mean **losing a growing season.** #risk
3. **The contract cascade.** The redesign forces changes across:
   - **[[HARDWARE_ENGINEER_SKILL|HW]] → [[EDGE_AI_ML_ENGINEER_SKILL|ML]]:** new sensor characterization data (new bands, new noise profile).
   - **[[HARDWARE_ENGINEER_SKILL|HW]] → [[FIRMWARE_ENGINEER_SKILL|FW]]:** new driver (register map, timing); board respin.
   - **[[EDGE_AI_ML_ENGINEER_SKILL|ML]] → [[FIRMWARE_ENGINEER_SKILL|FW]]:** the preprocessing **golden reference + test vectors** change → firmware-parity re-validation (FC-009 territory).
   - **[[EDGE_AI_ML_ENGINEER_SKILL|ML]]:** retrain/transfer-learn on the new modality, re-quantize to the arena budget, re-validate accuracy parity. The prior model **does not transfer** without revalidation.
   - **[[DATA_ENGINEER_SKILL|DATA]]:** if the telemetry band set changes, a telemetry-schema change → Schema-Change Coordination Process (FW §6.8) with version bump (FC-010 risk).
   - **OTA:** a new `hardware_id` profile in the OCM (FC-036 mis-tag risk if the new revision is mislabeled).
   - **[[SECURITY_ENGINEER_SKILL|SEC]]:** new `#sensor-physics` attack surface → new STRIDE threat model (FC-029 re-examined).
   - **[[QA_TEST_AUTOMATION_ENGINEER_SKILL|QA]]:** re-run HIL + the 32-chain robustness suite; **re-derive FC-001** for the new AFE.
   - **[[docs/fmea/SYSTEM_FMEA_V1|FMEA]]:** update trigger (b) "any new … platform" → re-derive the hardware-initiated chains and, if the product class shifts, the R3 recovery numbers.
4. **The pre-Planning ownership hole recurs.** Exactly the Phase 1 S1 finding returns: the *new-sensor cost-down* (to stay under the affordability ceiling) is a pre-Planning objective with **no staffed owner** until Hardware is re-tasked. A launch-blocking cost problem can again become a Development-stage surprise. #gap
5. **Governance verdict on the change itself.** Structurally, the change is **well-governed**: STRATEGIC ADR + BIA + ARB review + Security re-review + FMEA re-derivation is the correct machinery and every cascade edge has a named contract. The weakness is **latency, not control**: the quarterly gate cadence + board respin + retrain + full robustness-suite re-run is realistically a **multi-month** effort, and the seasonal window is unforgiving.

**Scenario C verdict.** The cascade is **correctly governed and correctly slow.** Every contract that must change has an owner and an ADR path; the FMEA and Security re-review triggers fire as designed. But the redesign collides with two structural realities the design acknowledges but does not solve: the **quarterly/seasonal metabolism mismatch** (EN-2) and the **unowned pre-Planning cost-down**. A supplier EOL is the canonical external dependency the org cannot prevent; the question is whether it can *absorb* one inside a season, and the honest answer is: **only if the EOL is discovered far enough ahead of the seasonal window to clear a quarterly gate cycle.** The governance is sound; the clock is the adversary.

## 7. Residual Gaps

### 7.1 Gaps Discovered During Simulation

These survived Part 1 (static analysis) and Part 2 (prior simulation) and were surfaced only by tracing concerns *across* stages. They are the most dangerous because they are no single role's contract to own.

- **G-1 — The Security Engineer is a non-delegable single point of failure for every consequential release.** Security-Relevant sign-off cannot go to the Deputy; AgriSpectra's launches, OTA model updates, and crypto changes are all Security-Relevant; Champions cannot sign. One human carries the entire blocking authority while also running post-launch watch and incident response. (§1.3) #critical
- **G-2 — The OTA incident machinery is crash-shaped, not erosion-shaped.** Every IC declaration trigger keys on loud failure (FAILED-state %, SLO breach). The keystone FC-022 and FC-031 produce *no* such signal, so the best-resourced response function never activates for the worst failures. (§2.2) #critical
- **G-3 — Incident response is physics-bounded.** A Critical firmware patch cannot reach 50k LoRaWAN devices inside the 7-day SLA (downlink duty cycle), and a physical vuln (FC-028) cannot be OTA-patched at all. The 7-day Critical SLA and the 10-day Security-Relevant sign-off SLA are mutually inconsistent. (§5.1) #critical
- **G-4 — The Architect is a single hub whose absence freezes structural decisions and FMEA ownership.** Survivable for 4 weeks, not for 4 months; the ARB-expansion fix is unexecuted. (§5.2)
- **G-5 — The pre-Planning cost-down has no owner**, and it recurs on every supplier change (Scenario C) as well as at initial S1. (§5.3, Phase 1 S1)
- **G-6 — The HW↔FW bring-up boundary has no shared Definition of Done**, and it is the origin of FC-001/FC-006 — the boundary that should own drift detection is the boundary with no contract. (Phase 1 S3)

### 7.2 Gaps Closed by Specification But Not Yet Realized

The 14 pending prompts (§6). The execution risk concentrates in the seven pure-design items, and *within* those, in the three that close Critical chains: the **F1 OTA watchdog** (FC-014/FC-031), the **fleet-scale surge test + jittered backoff** (FC-019/FC-035), and the **nine FMEA §7.1 "does-not-exist-today" controls** — above all the **absolute ground-truth anchor + B5 field-push** for FC-022/FC-001. These are named, scored, and owner-assigned; they are not built. The MACP (the cross-role agent enabler) and Attestation Phase 1 (the FC-029/HA-A3 closer) are buildable-today drafts whose non-execution leaves D = 9 silent chains open and the agent ecosystem uncoordinated.

### 7.3 Gaps That Cannot Be Closed by Design

These are inherent — the negative space between contracts, unverifiable assumptions, and external dependencies. They must be **accepted and managed**, not engineered away.

- **HA-A3 residual.** Attestation (even at its stretch Phase 4) proves the *digital* integrity of device reports; it cannot prove the *physical* measurement is true. Analog sensor spoofing and in-range AFE drift (the physical half of FC-001) sit forever outside attestation. The attestation spec says this plainly — "it does not make the physical world honest."
- **HA-H1 disclosure-dependence.** The Pre-Transfer Security Review fires only on the Researcher's self-tag. A surface the Researcher does not recognize as novel is never threat-modeled. No design closes the unknown-unknown; only culture and breadth of review reduce it.
- **The closed-loop epistemic core.** FC-022's root is that *any* monitor re-baselining against field data normalizes slow corruption. The absolute ground-truth anchor mitigates it — but a frozen golden validation set can itself drift out of representativeness over a 7-year field life. The anchor bounds the problem; it does not dissolve it.
- **External dependencies.** Supplier EOL (Scenario C), LoRaWAN downlink physics (G-3), upstream package mutability (FC-034), and the seasonal/quarterly metabolism mismatch (EN-2) are structural constraints of the device class and the market, not defects to fix.
- **Inter-contract negative space.** Inference-output *semantics* ownership is split between Architect-as-semantics-provider and Backend-as-data-provider; Planning Integration has no defined arbitration for mutually-incompatible plans. These are the seams between contracts, where no single §6 entry has the obligation — manageable by escalation, not closable by a clause.

## 8. Phase 2 Interim Verdict

**Cross-cutting readiness is CONDITIONAL — the same shape the Phase 1 stage verdicts converged on from S3 onward, and for the same reason: the architecture is sound, the map is honest and complete, and the territory the map describes is roughly half-walked.** Security is structurally embedded at every stage with no orphaned hand-off and a veto that survives emergency tempo — the strongest cross-cutting concern. OTA governance has a real single-source-of-truth contract and a correct closed loop, and the Incident Commander is a well-formed coordinator. Quality attributes are guaranteed-on-paper and, for five of six, conditionally-guaranteed-in-fact; the sixth (Robust) is the load-bearing gap, honestly self-reported at ≈ 53% detection coverage. AI-agent readiness is high per-role (the eight schemas are the hard part and they exist) and low cross-role (MACP, the conductor, is an unbuilt draft). **None of this requires structural redesign. All of it is named, scored, and owned.**

But the cross-cutting trace earns its keep by surfacing three gaps that no single stage verdict captures, because each spans stages and roles:

**Top 3 cross-cutting gaps that must be closed before GO:**
